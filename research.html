Broadly, I want to study how conversation shapes connection, identity, and influence:

  • How framing, disagreement, and power dynamics affect collaboration and mutual understanding
  • How people regulate emotion in group settings, and when that regulation becomes a personal cost
  • How expressions of confidence, identity, and emotion influence authenticity and social perception

Research In Progress

Wissinger, M., & Teeny, J. D. (2025). Relational Costs of Goal-Directed Listening in Workplace Conversations. (Data-collection phase)

Abstract:

Everyday conversations often demand that listeners balance accuracy with empathy. These competing motives reflect what Yeomans et al. (2022) describe as informational motives (exchanging accurate information) versus relational motives (building connection). We examine this tension in professional contexts. In an experiment (Connect, N = 196; Mage = 38.66, female = 50.5%, male = 49.0%, other = 0.5%), participants listened to standardized audio from an ostensible coworker. The coworker described a workplace challenge, and participants were randomly assigned to a relational goal or a non-relational goal (informational or control). Relationally assigned listeners who felt goal conflict (i.e., felt torn between being accurate and being supportive) reported lower authenticity (b = –0.24, p < .001), less willingness to reengage (b = –0.46, p = .004), and more emotional exhaustion (b = +0.53, p < .001) than those in the non-relational conditions who felt similar levels of goal conflict. Notably, Johnson–Neyman analysis indicated that this last effect on emotional exhaustion emerged even under low conflict values, affecting approximately 71% of the sample. These findings suggest that relational listening goals can leave employees depleted and less willing to reengage with colleagues, offering important insight for workplace interventions focused on interpersonal cohesion.
